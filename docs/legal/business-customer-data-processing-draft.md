# Business customer data processing schedule

Draft for review and completion before signature. The parties identify the business customer, the legal supplier trading as Vertex Seven, their contacts, the service agreement and its effective date in the signed order.

## Processing instructions

For customer conversations, the customer determines the purpose, which channels to enable, the people who can access them and which follow-up actions to approve. The supplier processes that data to operate the purchased service under the customer's documented instructions, including instructions about international transfers. If UK law requires other processing, the supplier will inform the customer before processing unless the law prohibits notice on important grounds of public interest. The supplier will tell the customer immediately if an instruction appears to infringe applicable data protection law.

This schedule applies to people contacting the customer and to staff whose information appears in the workspace. Depending on the features enabled, data can include names, contact details, messages, call transcripts, assistant replies, catalogue information, interaction identifiers, bookings, quotes and approval records. The operations include receiving, storing, retrieving, generating drafts, routing, sending approved or authorised responses, exporting and deleting data. The customer specifies its enabled features, permitted uses and retention settings in the order or workspace instructions. Separate platform purposes such as subscription billing and account administration are described in the platform privacy notice.

## Confidentiality and security

The supplier will restrict access to authorised people who need it and are bound by confidentiality. It will implement technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risks, maintain access controls and tenant separation, protect credentials and verify provider callbacks. The parties will record agreed measures and incident contacts in the order.

## Sub-processors and transfers

Before signature, attach the providers actually used for the customer, their functions, processing locations and applicable transfer safeguards. Under general written authorisation, the supplier will give advance written notice of intended additions or replacements and allow the customer to object before the change. The parties will record the notice period and objection procedure in the order. Each sub-processor must accept equivalent data protection obligations. The supplier remains liable to the customer for performance of those obligations. Restricted transfers require a lawful mechanism applicable to the route and any required assessment.

## Assistance and incidents

The supplier will promptly route individual rights requests to the customer and assist it through appropriate technical and organisational measures, taking the nature of processing into account. Taking account of the information available to it, the supplier will assist with security, breach notifications, impact assessments and prior consultation with the regulator. The supplier will notify the customer without undue delay after becoming aware of a personal data breach and supply available information as the investigation progresses.

## End of service and assurance

At the customer's choice, the supplier will return or delete the personal data after the processing service ends and delete existing copies unless UK law requires storage. Before signature the parties must agree an export method, deletion timetable and backup expiry consistent with actual operations. The supplier will make compliance information available and allow and contribute to audits and inspections by the customer or its appointed auditor.

The customer is responsible for its lawful instructions, privacy information, staff access and handling of rights requests. Both parties retain their statutory responsibilities. The parties must resolve the open order particulars, verify supplier arrangements and approve this schedule before relying on it.
